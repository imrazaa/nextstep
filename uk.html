Why the UK?

World-renowned universities with a clear path from graduation to work.

World-Class, Globally Recognised Universities

Home to some of the world's oldest and most respected universities, a UK degree carries strong recognition with employers and graduate schools worldwide.

Key Highlight

Graduate Route: 2 Years to Work After You Graduate

Graduates can apply to stay and work in the UK for up to 2 years (3 for PhD holders) under the Graduate visa, with no job offer or sponsor required to apply. Postgraduate research options like MRes can also let your spouse or children join you during your studies.

Huge South Asian Community

The UK is home to around 5 million people of South Asian heritage, including large Indian, Pakistani, and Bangladeshi communities, so you'll find familiar food, culture, and support networks in most major cities.

Work While You Study

Once your visa is granted, you can work up to 20 hours a week during term time and full-time during official holidays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need IELTS to study in the UK?

Most UK universities do require IELTS, commonly a minimum of 6.0 in each module (Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking), with stronger programs and postgraduate courses often asking for 6.5 or higher. A smaller number of universities accept alternatives, such as a Medium of Instruction (MOI) letter or strong school English grades, but this varies a lot by institution and program. For the visa itself, UK Visas and Immigration separately requires an approved Secure English Language Test (such as PTE Academic UKVI) unless you qualify for an exemption. We help you confirm the exact requirement for each university you apply to.

Can my spouse or children come with me to the UK?

It depends on the course. Under current rules, dependants are generally only allowed to join students on postgraduate research programs, such as an MRes, MPhil, or PhD, of 9 months or longer, most standard taught Master's (MSc, MA, MBA) no longer qualify. If bringing family is a priority for you, an MRes is often the strongest route, though universities can classify it differently for visa purposes, so we always confirm this with the specific program before you apply.

What proof of funds do I need for a UK student visa?

You'll need to show your remaining tuition balance (as shown on your CAS) plus maintenance funds for up to 9 months, currently around £1,529/month if studying in London or £1,171/month outside London, held for 28 consecutive days. We help you prepare the right documentation as part of our Advantage and Complete Packages.

How much does it cost to study in the UK?

International tuition typically ranges from around £11,400–£38,000 a year for Bachelor's and £9,000–£38,000 for Master's (most commonly £15,000–£25,000), alongside a student visa fee of £558 and the Immigration Health Surcharge of £776 per year of study.

Can I work while studying?

Students on degree-level courses can generally work up to 20 hours a week during term time and full-time during official university holidays, once their visa is granted.

What happens after I graduate?

Graduates can apply for the Graduate visa to stay and work in the UK for up to 2 years (3 for PhD holders), with no job offer or sponsor needed. Note that visa rules can change, current guidance allows the full 2-year period for applications made on or before 31 December 2026, so it's worth checking the latest rules closer to your intake.

How early should I start the process?

Ideally 8–12 months before your intended intake, to allow time for application deadlines, English proficiency documentation, and setting up your financial documentation and accommodation.
